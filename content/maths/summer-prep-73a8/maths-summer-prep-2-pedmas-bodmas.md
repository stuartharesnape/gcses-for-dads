## 3. BIDMAS, BODMAS or PEMDAS?

This is just a way of working out how to process an equation. Learn this and then be smug when you see thousands of people get it wrong on instagram. 

Your child may hear different names for the same rule depending on their school, teacher or where the resource comes from.  **It's the same thing.**

Dead simple.
- Do anything in brackets
- Then any thing with an exponent (like squared or square root)
- Then go left to right with multiplication or division
- Finally left to right with addition and subtraction


| BIDMAS | BODMAS | PEMDAS |
|--------|--------|--------|
| **B**rackets | **B**rackets | **P**arentheses |
| **I**ndices | **O**rders | **E**xponents |
| **D**ivision | **D**ivision | **M**ultiplication |
| **M**ultiplication | **M**ultiplication | **D**ivision |
| **A**ddition | **A**ddition | **A**ddition |
| **S**ubtraction | **S**ubtraction | **S**ubtraction |

### What's the difference?

- **BIDMAS** is the version most commonly taught in UK schools.
- **BODMAS** is also widely used. **Orders** means the same as **Indices** (powers).
- **PEMDAS** is the American version. **Parentheses** means brackets and **Exponents** means indices.

Whichever version you use, the answers are exactly the same.

### Example 1

```
2 + 3 × 4
```

Many people answer **20**.

The correct answer is **14** because you multiply first. So 3 times 4, then add 2.

---

### Example 2

```
10 - 6 ÷ 2
```

Many people answer **2**.

The correct answer is **7** because division happens before subtraction. So -6 divided by 2 (remember the negative) plus 10

---

### Example 3

```
(10 - 6) ÷ 2
```

Now the answer is **2** because the brackets come first.

The brackets completely change the calculation.

---

### Example 4

```
100 ÷ 10 × 2
```

Don't multiply first.

Division and multiplication have **equal priority.**

Just work from **left to right**.

```
100 ÷ 10 = 10

10 × 2 = 20
```

## Key Things to Watch

- Ignoring brackets.
- Forgetting to work from left to right when multiplying and dividing.
- Losing track of negative signs.
- Mixing up fractions, decimals and percentages.
- Reaching for a calculator when simple mental maths would do.

1. 8 + 3 × 6
