# Summer Prep 2: Number Skills

---

## 1. The Big Idea (30 seconds)

Being good at maths isn't about remembering lots of tricks.

It's about becoming comfortable with numbers.

Children who are confident with simple calculations usually find algebra, geometry and problem solving much easier because they aren't fighting the arithmetic at the same time.

---

## 2. Negative Numbers

Negative numbers are simply numbers below zero.

A number line is often the easiest way to think about them.

- Positive numbers are to the right of zero.
- Negative numbers are to the left.
- Adding moves right.
- Subtracting moves left.

Examples:

```
5 - 8 = -3

-4 + 7 = 3

-6 - 2 = -8
```

### Dad Tip

If your child gets stuck, don't guess.

- Draw a quick number line.

- It's surprising how often that makes the answer obvious.

---

## 3. Fractions, Decimals and Percentages

Fractions, decimals and percentages are simply different ways of writing the same number.

| Fraction | Decimal | Percentage |
|----------|---------|-----------|
| ½ | 0.5 | 50% |
| ¼ | 0.25 | 25% |
| ¾ | 0.75 | 75% |
| ⅕ | 0.2 | 20% |
| ¹⁄₁₀ | 0.1 | 10% |
| ⁸³⁄₁₀₀ | 0.83 | 83% |

The quicker your child can move between these, the easier GCSE Maths becomes.

I prefer to just use the decimal tbh. My kids got used to it too. So for example 72% of £150 just becomes £150 * 0.72 = £108.

### Dad Tip

Don't try to memorise hundreds of conversions.

Learn the common ones until they become automatic.

## 4. A Quick Fraction Refresher

Fractions are one of those things that are easy to forget.

This isn't a lesson on fractions. It's just a reminder of the basic rules.

### Adding Fractions

If the denominator is the same, add the numerators.

```
⅖ + ¹⁄₅ = ⅗
```

If the denominators are different, make them the same first.

```
½ + ¼

= ²⁄₄ + ¹⁄₄

= ¾
```

### Multiplying Fractions

Multiply the top numbers together.

Multiply the bottom numbers together.

```
⅔ × ¾

= ⁶⁄₁₂

= ½
```

Always simplify your answer if you can.

### Dividing Fractions

KFC = Chicken..er it means Keep Flip Change!

Keep the first fraction.

Flip the second fraction.

Multiply.

```
½ ÷ ¼

= ½ × ⁴⁄₁

= 2
```
