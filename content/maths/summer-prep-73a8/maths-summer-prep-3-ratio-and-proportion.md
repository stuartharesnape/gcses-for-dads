# GCSEs for Dads – Summer Prep 3: Ratio and Proportion

Ratio questions are everywhere in GCSE Maths.

Recipes, maps, probability, speed, scale drawings and algebra all rely on the same basic idea.

Get comfortable with ratios now and they'll become one of the easier topics later on.

| Skill | Key Idea | Meaning |
|-------|----------|---------|

| Simplifying | Divide by the highest common factor | Makes ratios easier to read |
| Sharing | Divide in proportion | Split an amount fairly using a ratio |

# Summer Prep 3: Ratio and Proportion

---

## 1. The Big Idea (30 seconds)

Ratio isn't about the actual numbers.

It's about the relationship between them.

If the relationship stays the same, the ratio stays the same.

---

## 2. Simplifying Ratios

Simplifying a ratio works just like simplifying a fraction.

Divide every part of the ratio by the same number.

Example:

```
12 : 18

Divide both by 6

2 : 3
```

Another example:

```
20 : 30 : 50

Divide all three by 10

2 : 3 : 5
```

### Dad Tip

Children often divide one side but forget to divide the others.

Every part of the ratio must be divided by the same number.

---

## 3. Sharing in a Ratio

A ratio tells you how many equal parts there are altogether.

Example:

```
Share £60 in the ratio 2 : 3
```

Step 1

Count the total parts.

```
2 + 3 = 5
```

Step 2

Work out one part.

```
£60 ÷ 5 = £12
```

Step 3

Multiply.

```
2 parts = £24

3 parts = £36
```

---

## 4. Scaling

Scaling means making something bigger or smaller while keeping everything in the same proportion.

Recipes are the easiest example.

```
Serves 4

400g flour

Need to serve 8

Double everything.

800g flour
```

Exactly the same idea is used in maps, models and scale drawings.

---

## 5. Best Buys

Ratio questions appear in real life more often than most people realise.

Example:

```
750g for £3.60

1kg for £4.40
```

Rather than guessing, work out the cost per kilogram.

The cheaper one is the better buy.

---

## Note from Dad

Ratio is one of those topics that suddenly clicks.

Children often struggle because they try to memorise methods instead of thinking about what the numbers mean.

If they're stuck, slow it down.

Ask questions like:

- What is the ratio comparing?
- How many parts are there altogether?
- What would one part be worth?

Most mistakes disappear once they answer those three questions.

---

## Key Things to Watch

- Only simplifying one side of a ratio.
- Forgetting to divide every part by the same number.
- Forgetting to add the ratio parts together before sharing.
- Comparing prices without making them the same size first.

---

## Check Your Understanding

1. Simplify 12 : 18
2. Simplify 15 : 25
3. Share £42 in the ratio 1 : 2
4. Share £84 in the ratio 3 : 4
5. A recipe serves 4 people using 500g of flour. How much flour is needed for 8 people?
6. A map has a scale of 1cm : 5km. How many kilometres does 8cm represent?
7. Which is the better buy: 500g for £2.20 or 750g for £3.00?
8. Simplify 8 : 12 : 20

### Answers

1. 2 : 3
2. 3 : 5
3. £14 and £28
4. £36 and £48
5. 1000g
6. 40km
7. 750g for £3.00
8. 2 : 3 : 5
